## 2. Source-level distinction and graph rule

- `RemeshPipeline.cpp:9848–9865` emits an all-rail, same-kind *closed* `SurfaceCellRail` using a complete feature curve. It does **not** necessarily split this rail where A2b later puts Trace terminals.
- `EmbeddedGraphTopology.cpp:430–557` independently records `MandatoryBarrierTermination` events and a `terminalBarrier` source edge, rejects two terminal cuts on one source edge, and emits **two original A2b Mandatory children** separated at their exact interior `NetworkNodeId` whenever one terminal falls on that source edge. The rail can remain `closed=true` while the *original A2b arc join graph* has a protected break inside one of its rail edges.
- Therefore **do not reject all adjacency merely because `rail.closed`**. Instead construct eligible *joins between original A2b arcs*, using exact rail-ordered source-edge adjacency, original oriented end-node/exact-point continuity, independently derived source/region/feature/side authority and all protective vetoes. Delete each candidate join across a Trace/Cut contact, feature corner, port, branch or other protected stop; unknown joins are ineligible. No positional welding or invented rail seam.
- Partition this **post-veto original-arc join graph** into maximal connected components. Certified degree ≤2 open components with **distinct topological endpoints and different `NetworkEdgeId` ancestry** may publish as oriented chains. A connected cyclic component with **no certified break** has no canonical open source-support span: retain its original arcs separately; do not choose a lowest-ID/row-index seam. A path whose *first and last source endpoints coincide* (e.g. just one protected stop around a ring) is still a closed/self-loop candidate and must not be promoted without separate cyclic authority. Applying these cases to `rail.closed` is a classification, **not** a global veto.
- In a ring with ≥2 independently verified protected cuts, removing the forbidden joins can create two or more **open arc paths**, even though their parent rail stays closed. Every such path must independently pass side-specific region/exterior walk, sheet/component, exact-carrier and unique rotation tests. A Trace-split *sibling pair at the protected contact* is never itself a valid join, but either sibling may legally join an adjacent different original network edge away from the contact.

At least one emitted open path must cover ≥2 **distinct original Mandatory network-edge IDs**; a blanket `if (rail.closed) skip` must fail this test. The exact original A2b arcs remain unchanged.
2. **Cut stop:** try joining the two A2b siblings at either Trace terminal. The independent validator must reject **at the protected endpoint**, rather than dropping those children from the partition.
3. **Uncut ring:** remove all protected stops while retaining a valid ring. The independently reconstructed eligible graph closes cyclically, so the partition must contain only certified separate originals, not an arbitrary full-ring open chain. No asserted reduction.
4. **Single stop:** a protected stop can yield an arc-level path with identical start/end source topology node. Reject its promotion as a self-returning chain until an explicitly approved cyclic-unit rule exists.
5. **Region/side or corner veto:** add one inconsistent owner, sheet or feature corner at a previously legal interior join; verify the resulting open path is split exactly at that join with no false maximality. Conversely, removal of a legitimate corner stop must be caught using the source feature authority, not an unchecked candidate corner vector.

## 5. Review correction: region identity is not physical-side identity (2026-11-09)

The side-walk WIP says "two region IDs must be distinct." That is conservative exclusion, **not a proven A3 invariant**. `GlobalConformityBaseline.cpp:343–363` creates a distinct `ConformityBoundaryIncidenceId{region, ordinal}` for each boundary occurrence and permits span degree one or two; `:375–383` avoids region self-union, but `:441–451` still forms a two-ended graph edge when both occurrences have the same region ID. Parity counts two occurrences, not two distinct region IDs.

A same-region **two-sided occurrence** is not a chain that **returns to its starting source point**. Same-region dual-side joins remain ineligible until independently certified as two different physical sides, two separate oppositely oriented ordered A2b boundary-occurrence walks and unique rotation continuation for each. Classify exclusions as uncertified, not intrinsically invalid A2b topology. A represented positive is needed before admitting same-region joins. The negative is physical-side aliasing, not equal region IDs.
