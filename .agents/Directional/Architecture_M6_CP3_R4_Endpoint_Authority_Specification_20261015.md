## Publisher evidence and RA-64.6 resolution

The producer defines four distinct entities: a whole hard-feature rail, the part of it covered by an individual front side, a canonical route of the actual carrier runs of that front side, and the source-face charts chosen to represent its endpoints. They cannot be substituted for one another.

At `src/geometry/SurfaceCellTracing.cpp`, `assign_open_front_boundary_authority` (around lines 12481–12690) traverses **every segment in `cell.boundaryPaths[side]`**, derives its authoritative source carrier from that segment's face and supported source edge, and flushes a carrier run whenever the edge changes. It rejects a nonconsecutive repetition of one carrier, disconnected consecutive parameters, a changed boundary kind, or invalid hard-rail ownership. It then publishes `CanonicalRoute::from_observed_steps`. Thus **`routeLength=1` means precisely that this front side's supplied segment path has one carrier run**; it is not an assertion that the *entire* hard-feature rail has one edge. A second carrier actually present in that path cannot be silently omitted by this publisher.

In the bounded-disk source producer (around lines 16770–17370), the accepted boundary subdivision supplies source-chart endpoints later copied into the cell corners, while the four `cell.boundaryPaths` are independently traced from their chart-side endpoints. An exact source vertex can legitimately be represented in a face incident to that **vertex** but not to the preceding **carrier edge**. A chart endpoint outside the carrier's two incident faces does not imply that the publisher omitted a carrier from the front-side path.

**What is established:** the two-carrier inference drawn in RA-64.6 solely from endpoint faces `{0,7}` and a one-step carrier `(1,4)` is unsound. Source construction permits interpretation (ii): an authentic one-carrier front side with its endpoint represented through another chart of the same vertex. The existing `oriented_carrier` predicate (around lines 19106–19114), which demands that both endpoint face IDs equal the one carrier's two incident faces, is the wrong *granularity* of authority for a vertex junction.

**What is not established:** static inspection of this loop does **not** prove the preceding chart-path producer generated every intended segment in each of the 44 executed cases. A future route-truncation claim (interpretation (i)) must exhibit a specific missing **segment carrier** or a mismatch between the accepted-boundary endpoint coverage and the constructed `cell.boundaryPaths[side]`. No additional route step may be invented from endpoint face IDs alone. No existing RED has been reclassified as a runtime PASS.

## Producer-attested point-level contract

The first consumer of a paired hard-rail front side must receive or independently derive **one exact canonical source point for each physical endpoint**. The contract is conceptual, not a mandate to name a new type after this turn:

- Producer-owned rail, source component/local sheet, selected branch and oriented filled side, boundary occurrence, network arc/`ConformitySpanId`, **endpoint-specific** exact breakpoint ordinal, and validated source-support piece.
- Canonical source-edge topology and exact rational edge parameter at that breakpoint, normalized to a canonical source vertex when it coincides exactly with an edge endpoint. Retain start/end direction and the correct front-side ownership. Neither a whole-rail terminal set nor a generic edge-record interval is an endpoint certificate.
- Actual source-face occurrence and trace barycentric position, checked as a representation of that exact source entity, never a competing source of point identity. It must be legal for different endpoint charts to describe the same source vertex.
- Checked chart-to-chart sector transport and A3 path evidence binding the exact source point to both selected front-side attachments. The selected directed filled side and branch remain authoritative throughout.

The existing A2b conformity producer already owns the requisite exact calculation. `GlobalConformityBaselinePlan::breakpoint_location(span, exactOrdinal)` supplies the checked support-piece index and exact local numerator/denominator. The source `append_arc` computes an **exact rational** source-edge parameter from this location and `arcSchedule.first/second`, **then** converts to a double when emitting `boundary_trace_point`; it publishes the exact span, ordinals, orientation, and occurrence in `SurfaceSharedBoundaryInterval` (around lines 16990–17120). A later implementation should expose **that same checked producer product**, retaining its actual endpoint-to-support-piece binding, rather than reconstructing point identity from double barycentrics or accepting caller-supplied source-point keys. Failure to prove the binding is a typed rejection, not permission for fallback.

Matching arc spans and reverse ordinals alone prove **edge-record selection**, not point identity. An identical actual producer-owned arc and support piece plus independently resolved exact parameters at **each corresponding endpoint** is the required stronger witness. The A4 verifier must independently check that binding against real source topology and chart occurrences. No epsilon, rounded world position, approximate chart adjacency, or equality of independently rounded double barycentric values can establish exact point identity. This is not withdrawn RA-61.9 under a new label.

## Ordered verification and first-rejection ladder

**A — selected front and segment authority.** Require a source-owned hard rail and component/sheet, matching A1 selected branch and oriented filled side, a consistent source-backed A2b/A3 producer, actual reverse paired front sides, source-owned arc/span, exactly reciprocal endpoint ordinals, opposite orientation, and a complete per-side segment-to-route inventory. Missing ownership, mismatched selected side/rail, non-hard edge, nonreciprocal pair, incomplete path, or ambiguous region must reject *before* an endpoint interpretation.

**B — exact endpoint identity.** Resolve the two *specific* physical endpoints independently from each half's validated A2b breakpoint location and support. Check equal canonical source entities and exact parameters at corresponding reversed ordinals. Check that each actual source-face chart occurrence represents that source point in the same component/sheet. Reject a mismatched exact point even if spans and ordinals are reciprocal. When the producer did not retain sufficient data to connect an endpoint to an exact source point, the contract must be amended **at its owner producer stage** in a later separately authorized turn, not guessed downstream.

**C — directed chart transport.** Strict edge-interior contact must preserve its source-carrier membership, exact edge parameter, and orientation. At an exact source vertex, allow a source-face chart not incident to the carrier edge **only through a source-attested, uniquely admissible vertex-star route** at that same vertex. Require valid A3 transitions, unchanged selected branch, directed side and source sheet, exact source-chart connections, non-hard route restrictions, sector disjointness, and a unique orientation/sector assignment. Reject if no route exists, multiple admissible routes remain, the route crosses a forbidden rail, or the alleged vertex is foreign. A whole-rail terminal pair cannot authenticate an interior feature junction.

**D — complete route and topology.** Validate actual path/route carrier correspondence, order, continuity, boundary subdivisions and endpoint incidence, reverse pairing, no duplicated carrier or hidden jump, oriented ownership, and all pre-existing singularity/holonomy/topology gates. A path traversing two different actual carriers with a route reporting one must reject; a legitimate one-carrier path ending in a different chart of a shared vertex must not acquire a fabricated second route step. Preserve final fail-closed validation and forbid rescue by downstream source-grid or legacy fallback.

Every gate must identify its **first** failing predicate and exact responsible product. A4 may authenticate a published A2b/A3 proof; it may not manufacture an A2b breakpoint, claim missing selected-side authority, guess a source transition, or weaken the original hard-rail guards.
