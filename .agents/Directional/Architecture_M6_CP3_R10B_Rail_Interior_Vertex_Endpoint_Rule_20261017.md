## 1. Decision: independently published open-rail chain, not two-spoke inference

**Choose the existing ordered upstream rail chain.** `SurfaceCellRail::sourceVertices/sourceEdges` (H:104–115) is built by `build_authoritative_surface_cell_rails` (P:9258,12336) before A4's endpoint certificate. It reaches the consumer as `SurfaceCellTracingOptions::authoritativeRails` (H:2246, P:12476, S:19059), but the consumer does not presently inspect it at endpoint admission. The verifier instead receives `hard_rail_source_provenance_from_rails` (P:9732–9767), which retains an entire chain for closed rails (P:9741–9754), yet publishes only first/last open-rail contact vertices (P:9756–9765). `publish_phase_front_result` passes only those projected objects to `SurfacePhaseFrontProduct::make` (S:12832–12869; factory S:8223–8240; H:1916–1917).

Define a distinct **source-owned ordered open-rail chain** record keyed by `HardRailId`, containing exact typed vertices, consecutive exact `SourceEdgeTopologyKey` identities, component, and `closed=false`. The helper's projection must retain this record *in addition to* the two existing contact terminals. The consumer derives it from `authoritativeRails`; the verifier gets it through the checked factory's explicit input, stores it on the checked product if downstream consistency is needed, and **revalidates it against its independently provided source topology and hard-feature edges**. The two checkers cannot rely on `route.oriented_steps()`, an A4 certificate field, or the candidate's own junctions to prove named-rail membership.

**Admissible chain witness** (all required): unique `railId` and nonclosed chain with at least three distinct, ordered vertices; every consecutive edge exists in the source as a hard-feature edge on the stated component/sheet; each consecutive pair has exact incidence; no repeated vertices/edges, branch ambiguity, foreign component, ambiguous rail overlap, or inconsistent published contact endpoints; `sourceEdges` exactly agrees with consecutive `sourceVertices`; the R vertex is at position `1..n-2`, occurs exactly once, and its *two* immediate chain edges are the named rail's hard spokes. The claimed carrier must equal **one** of those two edges, and the paired front's recorded route must be a contiguous directed chain subinterval (or its exact reversal), with endpoint positions and intermediate source-edge order consistent. This last requirement applies to **R-bearing routes** and does not retroactively change the accepted T–T / T–I branch. An independent source-entity/edge-component verification must fail closed if provenance cannot establish any clause. Sources: R10A table 2 “named front rail,” “R belongs,” “both incident hard edges,” “carrier,” “trace points”; H:104–115, H:1519–1525, S:8528–8569, S:19068–19081, P:9732–9767.

**Classification:** this is a **consumer/verifier authority-plumbing repair**, not mere earlier rejection relocation (RA-72.5): a previously available independent upstream chain is preserved across the verifier boundary and enables a *new, falsifiable* R admission. It is **not** Gate C's forbidden pre-admission, rail-scoped **producer-authored vertex-star certificate** (RA-65.5, RA-68.3, clarified RA-78.4): the chain originates before A4 and establishes only source-rail membership, never A3 sector-path uniqueness. Gate C remains suspended; no new A4 authority is licensed. If upstream chain independence or source-identity validation fails, reject R rather than inferring ownership from two spokes.

## 2. Exactly one endpoint predicate in both checkers

Apply the following disjoint case classification and conjunction to *each paired directed-side endpoint*, using original attachment faces and points, not their 3D proximity. The constructor (`S:19300–19531`) and factory verifier (`S:8658–8765`) must independently derive the same decision from source authority:

1. **T (unchanged).** Both side points decode to one identical, **published** terminal source vertex using exact one-hot local barycentrics; vertex is a contact endpoint and endpoint of the named carrier; source component matches. Require exactly one admissible carrier orientation, one `Unique` non-hard-spoke A3 star path **per side**, reciprocal A3, and disjoint directed-side face sectors. These are R9B V clauses; sources: R10A table 2 “R is not contact terminal,” “trace points,” “one unique path,” “correct orientation”; S:19347–19366, 19433–19531, 8683–8765.
2. **R (new).** Neither point claims T; both points decode the **same exact one-hot SourceVertexId R** (each in its own real incident source face) and independent *named open-rail chain* membership from §1 proves R is strictly internal, with both named consecutive hard spokes. The endpoint carrier is one of those two hard rail edges, from the exact published transition, with compatible component/sheet, paired route, shared-boundary interval and reciprocal orientation. For **each side**, independently find exactly **one** non-hard-feature A3 path from its actual attachment face to its selected carrier face through the source star of R; both sides' visited-face sectors (including starts) must be disjoint; **exactly one** of the two carrier orientations may satisfy all predicates. Never infer a path from chain membership, accept a nonincident/ambiguous path, or cross a hard spoke. Sources: R10A table 2 “R belongs,” “both incident hard edges,” “carrier,” “both trace points,” “one unique path,” “orientation/disjoint”; S:8166–8220, 8495–8526, 8658–8765, 19393–19531; H:1482–1499. The verifier must prepopulate source-star incidence for independently witnessed R vertices as well as T vertices (S:8495–8519), recompute `unique_terminal_star_path` (semantically a *vertex*-star path) and retain `Missing`/`Ambiguous`/`Invalid` as hard failures. Do not copy an A4-supplied star certificate into authority.
3. **I (unchanged).** Neither point is a witnessed exact T or R; both are strictly edge-interior on the identical canonical published carrier and have exactly equal `FieldExactRational` carrier parameters; attachment faces equal the oriented carrier pair, contact paths empty, interval/ordinal/route checks unchanged. A vertex at a carrier endpoint never becomes I. Sources: R10A table 2 “trace points,” “carrier”; S:64–97, 19372–19388, 8699–8711. The 324 observed one-ULP differences remain rejected.
4. **C (unchanged, disjoint).** Contact-free closed rails require their independent cyclic source-chain and existing literal carrier-face rules; there are **no** terminal or R claims for closed rails. Sources: S:19325–19341, 8729–8735; P:9741–9754.

Represent T / R / I distinctly in the future checked endpoint product (e.g. typed endpoint kind with `SourceVertexId` for T/R). Do **not** overload `terminalVertex` (H:1492–1499) with an R vertex; a verifier must reject mismatched/multiple tags. `terminalCarrier` can retain its current field for ABI stability in a separate implementation review; its name grants no terminal authority. The two checkers must enforce identical classification priority, exact source-entity conditions, component/sheet validity, oriented A3 requirements, unique path counts, sector exclusion, and paired interval authority; the verifier must recompute these independently rather than trusting certificate paths (S:8528–8569, 8621–8765).

## 3. Route-level anchor and the I–I asymmetry

For an **open** rail, first preserve the existing opposite-T–T terminal-pair direction condition **unchanged** (`S:8581–8615`); preserve existing T–I single-terminal admission **unchanged**. Then allow a route *without a T end* **only when** at least one endpoint is independently certified R by §1–2, its contiguous ordered-chain interval is certified, and every other endpoint independently passes its T/R/I predicate with matching paired route, rail, component and carrier. Thus **I–R** and **R–R** can pass conditionally, not automatically. **T–R** can pass under its T anchor only if the R-specific chain and path tests pass. **I–I must be rejected by both**: neither provides a T anchor nor the new R chain-vertex anchor. The verifier must not treat mere non-null `terminalVertex` as T; it must check the typed case against the independent contact/chain source. Sources: R10A table 1 I–I / T–I / R–R; RA-83.2; S:8574–8615, 8699–8721, 19397–19425, 20234–20245, 12858–12869.

Before the consumer appends its certificate (`S:20243–20245`), it must enforce **the same route-anchor rule** that the factory independently checks before its per-endpoint loop (`S:8574–8615`), closing RA-83.2's consumer-admits / publication-rejects I–I gap. A rejected route cannot reach `SurfacePhaseFrontProduct::make` as a locally certified front. If a later generalization wishes to accept pure I–I interior subsegments, it needs a separately reviewed independent exact subsegment/phase witness: this definition does **not** invent one.
