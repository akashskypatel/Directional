## Source-derived producer finding

In `src/geometry/PureQuadCompletion.cpp`, `complete_rectangular_grid` constructs a width × height grid from four corner provenance positions, choosing an interior target by corner-only bilinear interpolation. It does **not** interpolate the intermediate vertices of its four actual boundary sides. Each interior target is passed to `append_embedded_vertex` → `project_generated_point`, which uses `SurfaceProjectionBvh::project(target, allowedFaces)` and can select a closest allowed source face; no injective patch-coordinate mapping or cell-order certificate is consulted at that point. It then publishes all width × height quads and marks the backend `ClosedForm`. Only later does `completed_quads_have_simple_embedding` reject bad quads, and failure does not re-enter an alternative grid construction. The `completionVariant` selection is not consumed by this grid producer. Thus retrying rotation/reversal variants does not generate a different structured grid.

**Independent counterexample to the corner-only premise, not a reproduction of Vase:** Let the simple planar CCW eight-vertex patch boundary be
`[(0,0),(.5,.75),(1,0),(1,.5),(1,1),(.5,1),(0,1),(0,.5)]` with side counts (2,2,2,2). Its corner-only bilinear interior point is `(.5,.5)`, outside the patch, and the two lower grid cells are invalid/self-intersecting. A side-aware transfinite interpolation places it at `(.5,.875)`, inside the patch, and makes the four constructed planar cells simple and contained. This was independently checked with planar polygon predicates; it is an existence counterexample, not a proof that transfinite interpolation always works on curved or concave patches. It isolates a genuine deficiency in the target construction **even without any surface projection**.

Therefore it cannot establish whether the *first* Vase fold was already in the bilinear target, introduced by closest-face projection, or caused by a rectangular chart that cannot embed without different topology.
