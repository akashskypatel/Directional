**CB31-REV-F1 — inverse-arm tamper is meaningful but its expected directed sign is not independently derived.** `OddAtlasWrongDirectedInverseRejectsAtCanonicalEdge` changes the raw cross arms (`inverseArm=true`) to a valid inverse-oriented *different* field, obtains an internally reciprocal A3 atlas, positively admits that atlas, then supplies the original face-gauge vector. Its asserted inverse relationship, however, is calculated exclusively from two atlases produced by the code under review (`forward->transport.inverse() == inverse->transport`), without an expected signed quarter-turn independently derived from the raw arms and source-face orientation. A common systematic sign or row-key error could satisfy the relationship and rejection even though the sign is incorrect. Both negatives target gauge-pin consistency; neither independently proves directed-sign correctness or invalid reciprocal reverse semantics. The review does **not** require forging a nonreciprocal typed atlas, which may be forbidden by the authoritative A3 constructor.

**CB31-REV-F2 — canonical permutation checks do not independently constrain expected raw-to-topology gauge values.** `odd_atlas_owner_draft` fills every expected gauge from `forward->transport.value()` of the atlas being tested. The valid row/cyclic-corner test checks the recalculated gauge vector against the factory's echoed vector and compares `periodicHolonomies()` / edge `periodicRelation` copied from the original fixture draft. These assertions are meaningful for factory admission and the stated copied owner projection, but do not establish that source-face topology keys were independently mapped to expected signed raw-field gauges. A consistently wrong atlas/source-row correspondence could supply both the fixture's expectation and its proof. The precommitted topology-keyed independent oracle is not established.
